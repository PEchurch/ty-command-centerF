## 1. The Core Idea for the Book

Both Hill and Yogananda teach that a disciplined, focused mind shapes the life you live. They aim that power at different targets:

- **Hill** points the mind at a definite goal, usually money.
- **Yogananda** points it at God, and treats success and prosperity as side effects of inner realization.

**Positive Energy sits between them.** The church's teaching already says prosperity is positive energy, not possession. Ty's own vision adds Hill's drive: a billion-dollar church, fueled by businesses, built through daily organized labor. The book can be the bridge: *use Yogananda for the why and the inner life, and Hill for the how and the outer plan, with Positive Energy as the way of life that holds both.*

## 2. Think and Grow Rich (Napoleon Hill, 1937)

### Background
- Published 1937 by the Ralston Society, first printing 5,000 copies. Working title: *The 13 Steps to Riches*.
- Condenses the 15 or 16 laws of Hill's earlier course, *The Law of Success* (1928), into 13 principles.
- The copyright was not renewed, so the book is in the public domain and can be quoted freely.
- Sales claims run from 20 million to over 100 million copies. None are audited.

### The 13 steps
1. **Desire.** A burning, specific want, written down with an amount, a deadline and what you will give for it.
2. **Faith.** Belief that you will get it, built on purpose.
3. **Autosuggestion.** Read your written goal aloud twice a day, with feeling.
4. **Specialized knowledge.** Organized, usable knowledge, often borrowed from experts.
5. **Imagination.** Synthetic imagination recombines old ideas. Creative imagination receives new ones from "Infinite Intelligence."
6. **Organized planning.** A concrete plan, built with others and revised when it fails.
7. **Decision.** Decide fast, change slowly.
8. **Persistence.** Sustained effort. Hill calls it what turns desire into faith.
9. **The Master Mind.** Two or more minds in harmony create a "third mind."
10. **Sex transmutation.** Redirect sexual energy into creative drive.
11. **The subconscious mind.** The link to Infinite Intelligence.
12. **The brain.** A "broadcasting and receiving station" for thought.
13. **The sixth sense.** Intuition and hunches, the "apex" of the philosophy. Hill says it opens through meditation.

Plus the closing chapter on the six ghosts of fear: poverty, criticism, ill health, loss of love, old age and death.

### From Hill's 1954 "Master Key" lectures
- The two sealed envelopes: **Riches** (sound health, peace of mind, a labor of love, freedom from fear and worry, a positive mental attitude, material riches) versus **Penalties** (ill health, fear, indecision, frustration, poverty, envy and hatred).
- The QQMA formula: Quality + Quantity of service + Mental Attitude decides your place and your pay.
- The gratitude prayer: ask not for more riches, but for wisdom to use well the gift of controlling your own mind.
- The central line: "Whatever the mind can conceive and believe, the mind can achieve."

### Credibility notes (use honestly in the book)
- Carnegie biographer David Nasaw found no evidence that Hill and Carnegie ever met. Hill told the story only after Carnegie died in 1919. In 1908 Hill's lumber company faced bankruptcy and fraud charges.
- The principles can stand on their own merits. Goal-setting research (Edwin Locke) supports specific written goals. The "500 millionaires" research claim should not be presented as evidence.

## 3. Autobiography of a Yogi (Paramahansa Yogananda, 1946)

### Background
- Yogananda (born Mukunda Lal Ghosh, Gorakhpur, India, 1893; died 1952) came to America in 1920, settled in Los Angeles in 1925 and founded Self-Realization Fellowship.
- In 1927 he became the first prominent Indian hosted at the White House.
- The book has been translated into more than 50 languages. Admirers include Steve Jobs, George Harrison and Elvis Presley.

### Core teachings
- **Self-realization is the goal.** You are a soul, one with God. Life's purpose is to know that directly.
- **Kriya Yoga is the method.** Chapter 26 presents it as a breath-and-energy technique that speeds spiritual growth.
- **Karma can be outwitted.** Chapter 16: the stars show your karmic pattern, and a will in tune with God rises above it.
- **The universe is made of thought and light.** Chapter 30, "The Law of Miracles."
- **Trust God for your needs.** Chapter 11, "Two Penniless Boys in Brindaban."
- **Unity of religions.** Krishna and Christ side by side; teachers of many faiths honored.

### Yogananda's own success teaching (*The Law of Success*)
- Positive thinking, dynamic will, self-analysis and meditation.
- Mind is the creator of everything. A thought held with strong will takes outward form.
- Habits of thought act as mental magnets.
- His conditions: first make sure what you want is right for you and in harmony with God. Work counts as success only when it serves others.

### Credibility notes
- The miracle stories are the most criticized part of the book. Philosopher David Lane argues most have ordinary explanations.
- Scholar Anya Foxen shows Yogananda blended Indian yoga with American New Thought, Unitarianism and Theosophy.

## 4. Where Hill and Yogananda Agree

- **Thought is causal.** Habitual thoughts act like magnets.
- **The mind is your one true possession.**
- **Habit is the battlefield.**
- **A higher intelligence exists, and you can tune in.** Hill's sixth sense; Yogananda's intuition.
- **Desire and sexual energy are power to be redirected.**
- **Adversity is a teacher.** Hill's "seed of an equivalent benefit"; Yogananda's karma lessons.
- **A shared American root in New Thought.** They even share a hero: Luther Burbank is one of Hill's nine imagined "Invisible Counselors," and Yogananda dedicated his autobiography to Burbank.

## 5. Where They Part Ways

| Question | Hill | Yogananda |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direction of the power | Spiritual serves material | Material serves spiritual |
| Desire | Build it into an obsession | Check it against God's will; want less as you grow |
| Who sets the goal | You choose it | The deepest goal is given: know God |
| Method | Written goals, plans, persistence, mastermind | Meditation passed from teacher to student |
| Authority | The self-made achiever | The realized master and guru lineage |
| Fear | Defeat it to get rich | It dissolves as you realize the soul is eternal |
| Success | Reaching your set aim | Inner joy and service to others |

## 6. Using Both

1. **Purpose first, then the plan.** Use Yogananda's test (is this right for me, and does it serve others?), then write the goal with Hill's specifics: what, by when, and what you'll give.
2. **Pair affirmation with stillness.** Read your goal aloud (Hill), then sit in silence (Yogananda). Answers surface when a problem is allowed to rest, which is also how Hill says hunches arrive.
3. **Gratitude as the bridge.** Hill's gratitude prayer, which asks for wisdom rather than more riches, is the meeting point of the two.
4. **Mastermind plus mentor.** Build Hill's circle of peers, and also seek one teacher further down the road.
5. **Read both critically.** Take the principles on their merits. Neither Hill's Carnegie story nor Yogananda's miracle stories proves anything.

## 8. The Key Tension for the Book to Resolve

Ty's written vision: *"I have built a billion dollar church that shares the power of Positive Energy with the world."*

The PE guide, Chapter 33: *prosperity is not possession.*

These do not cancel each other out. They are the book's central argument waiting to be written:

- **Hill's side:** a definite number and a deadline focus the mind. A billion dollars is a Hill-style "definite major purpose."
- **Yogananda's side:** the goal is right only if it serves others and fits a higher purpose.
- **The PE resolution:** wealth is fuel, not the destination. Ty's notes say it directly: the businesses exist "to build the wealth that will fuel the Church." The money is measured by what it makes possible, in line with the handbook's rule that success is measured by impact.

**Possible chapter title:** "Money Is Fuel, Not the Destination."

## 11. What May Be Overlooked (to check)

- **Tight deadline:** the websites are due November 1, eight days away.
- **Giving season anchors:** Native American Heritage Month (all of November), GivingTuesday on December 1, 2026, and year-end giving through December 31.
- **Money and legal structure:** keep church and business money separate. Income from a church-owned business can create tax obligations (unrelated business income tax). Have a CPA and an attorney review the mission-locked subsidiary and trust plan.
- **Rights:** confirm licensing for all music, footage and images in the films and broadcasts.
- **Film festivals:** list target festivals and their submission deadlines and fees now, since many deadlines fall months before the festival.
- **Marketing events calendar:** build a 2027 list of powwows, festivals, cultural holidays and markets in Oklahoma and online, with dates confirmed. Examples worth checking: the Cherokee National Holiday (Labor Day weekend, Tahlequah), Juneteenth, and Bare Bones Film Festival dates.
- **Metrics:** track a few numbers monthly: members of The Beat, email list size, store sales, donations, and acts of service.
- **Rest and delegation:** the PE guide's chapter on sacred rest, and Hill's Master Mind, both warn against carrying everything alone.
