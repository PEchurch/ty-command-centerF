## 5. Captions and accessibility
- Every video ships with accurate captions before it goes public. Auto-generated captions can be a first draft but need a human pass, especially for names and Cherokee words; university accessibility guidance says auto-captions are not enough for recorded video ([Berkeley DAP live captioning FAQ](https://dap.berkeley.edu/learn/techniques/live-captioning-faq); guidance from a university, not a legal standard).
- Workflow: auto-draft, human correction (Thu), export SRT, attach before scheduling. On YouTube-type hosts a caption file with timing can be uploaded in Studio under Subtitles; SRT is among supported formats ([JMU guide](https://www.jmu.edu/accessibility/digital-accessibility/guides/captioning/youtube/upload-a-captioning-file.shtml); third-party, unverified against the current official page). If the site self-hosts, a WebVTT/SRT track in the player is needed (unverified; check with web-launch-lead).
- Publish a text transcript on the video page. Describe on-screen graphics aloud. High-contrast lower thirds and large type.
- Spelling list: keep a glossary of Cherokee words, names, and Pillar terms for the caption corrector; Ty reviews pronunciation and spelling. Cultural content: consult before sharing songs, stories, or ceremony (production/rights-tracker.md).
- Whether the church has a legal accessibility obligation for its website is an attorney question (flag).
