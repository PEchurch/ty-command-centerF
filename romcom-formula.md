# The Rom-Com Formula
*Reverse-engineered from the 11 highest-grossing romantic comedies of 2006–2026*

> **About the numbers:** Box office figures are approximate worldwide grosses, written from memory and not checked against a live source. Verify them on Box Office Mojo or The Numbers before using them in a pitch.

## Part 2 — The Ten Success Drivers

1. **A premise you can explain in one sentence.** A forced-proximity device (a fake relationship, a pregnancy, a wedding, a deadline) traps two incompatible people together, so the conflict comes built in.
2. **A place the audience wants to visit.** 8 of 11 sell a lifestyle or location fantasy: Greece, Singapore, Sydney, Manhattan, Alaska.
3. **A female core audience that brings others.** Mother-daughter outings, girls' nights, date nights. The best performers also give men a reason to come.
4. **Proven stars and fresh pairings.** One bankable star, or two rising stars with real chemistry. Leads over 40 are an advantage.
5. **Something the audience already knows.** 7 of 11 adapt existing material: a TV show, ABBA songs, a novel, Shakespeare.
6. **A big supporting cast.** Family, friends and rivals carry much of the comedy.
7. **Music.** A song moment people share (ABBA; "Unwritten" in *Anyone But You*) keeps selling the film after release.
8. **Mid-sized budgets.** The sweet spot is $25–60M. The two films that spent $85M or more had the weakest returns.
9. **Release dates away from the blockbusters.** Summer counter-programming or the holiday-to-Valentine's window, then a long run on word of mouth. *Anyone But You* opened at about $6M domestic and grew for weeks on TikTok.
10. **A big emotional finish.** A public declaration, a race against the clock, or a wedding.

## Part 3 — The Formula

### Logline equation
> **[Two leads who seem wrong for each other] are forced together by [a contract, deception or deadline] in [a place people want to visit], while [family or friends] push the stakes higher, until [one public moment] forces the truth out.**

### Scorecard (greenlight at 8/10 or higher)
| Ingredient | Target |
|---|---|
| Pitchable in one line | A forced-proximity trope |
| Destination setting | Somewhere viewers would put on a vacation board |
| Familiar material | A book, a public-domain play, a song catalog, or a trope everyone recognizes |
| Casting | One bankable star plus one rising star, or two rising stars with chemistry |
| Comic supporting cast | 3+ memorable side characters, including a parent or family figure |
| Music moment | One song scene built to be clipped and shared |
| Reason for men to come | Physical comedy, edge or a sports/action hook |
| Appeal across generations | Roles that work for viewers over 40 |
| Grand-gesture ending | A public declaration or a race against the clock |
| Disciplined budget | $25–60M |

### Story structure (in minutes of runtime)
| Minutes | Story beat |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Establish the destination and show what each lead is missing |
| 10–25 | They meet, sparks and dislike are both clear, and the forced-proximity device locks them in |
| 25–55 | Banter, set pieces, family complications, and the first glimpse of who each really is |
| 55–65 | The midpoint: a moment of real vulnerability (a dance, a song, a night swim) |
| 65–80 | The lie or contract comes out and they break apart |
| 80–95 | One of them realizes the truth, the supporting cast pushes them, and the grand gesture happens |
| End | The music moment returns and the couple is happy, with a closing beat clipped for trailers and social media |

### Release and marketing plan
- **Release dates:** mid-December (to run through Valentine's Day) or early summer opposite a superhero film.
- **Social media:** design one clip-ready scene or song in development, not after the film is finished.
- **Ticket sales:** promote group and girls'-night outings and premium formats with cocktails.

## Part 4 — Three Original Loglines Built on the Formula

### 1. *Terms & Conditions*
**Logline:** A ruthless prenup attorney is secretly hired to break up her best friend's whirlwind engagement. The groom's equally ruthless best man is secretly hired to protect it. Both are stuck at the same ten-day villa wedding in Puglia, Italy, and the only way to win is to out-charm each other.

- **Trope:** Enemies to lovers with rival secret agendas (a nod to the public-domain *Much Ado About Nothing*)
- **Destination:** Whitewashed villas, olive groves and the Adriatic coast
- **Supporting cast:** The bride's twice-divorced mother; the groom's overbearing Italian nonna; a scheming wedding planner
- **Music moment:** The couple is pulled into a tarantella at the rehearsal dinner and dances it so well that the clip goes viral
- **Reason for men to come:** A rivalry of escalating pranks and sabotage
- **Grand gesture:** She stands up at the ceremony to object, and does it to declare her own feelings
- **Release:** Early summer counter-programming · **Budget target:** ~$35M
- **Score:** 9/10 (no existing IP beyond the Shakespeare nod)

### 2. *Half Interest*
**Logline:** A burned-out Chicago chef inherits half of her late aunt's failing beachfront cantina in Baja. The other half belongs to the laid-back surf instructor who has been running it into the ground. The will says neither of them can sell for one season.

- **Trope:** A forced partnership through an inheritance, with a clash of lifestyles
- **Destination:** The Baja California coast: surf, sunsets, fish tacos
- **Supporting cast:** Her divorced mother, who visits and falls for the town's mariachi bandleader (a B-plot for viewers over 40); a rival resort developer; the cantina's three-generation kitchen crew
- **Music moment:** The mother's mariachi serenade on the beach, later reprised at the cantina's grand reopening
- **Reason for men to come:** A surf-competition set piece and a cook-off against the resort
- **Grand gesture:** She turns down the developer's buyout on the night of the reopening, in front of the whole town
- **Release:** Valentine's Day weekend · **Budget target:** ~$30M
- **Score:** 9/10 (strong two-generation appeal; fits a book-to-film adaptation if there's a novel to license)

### 3. *Fake It Till Christmas*
**Logline:** A pop star whose image is falling apart needs a wholesome scandal-free romance before her holiday tour. She fake-dates her childhood neighbor, now a small-town Vermont firefighter, through the town's twelve-day winter festival while her label's documentary crew films every moment.

- **Trope:** A fake relationship for publicity, between celebrity and a hometown love
- **Destination:** A snowy New England village with markets, a skating pond and a covered bridge
- **Supporting cast:** Her pushy manager; his firehouse crew, who bet on how long it will last; both their mothers, who are secretly planning the wedding
- **Music moment:** An original song she writes about him, performed live at the tree lighting. It's built to become an actual seasonal hit
- **Reason for men to come:** Firehouse comedy and a hockey game on the frozen pond
- **Grand gesture:** She skips her tour opener and plays the town's Christmas Eve concert instead, live-streamed
- **Release:** Mid-December, running through Valentine's Day · **Budget target:** ~$40M
- **Score:** 10/10 (the soundtrack doubles as IP; a holiday title can be re-released every year)

## Part 5 — One-Page Pitch Template

Copy this for each new concept.

```
TITLE:
LOGLINE (one sentence, using the equation):

1. HOOK / TROPE:            ______  (forced proximity: fake dating / inheritance / wedding / deadline)
2. DESTINATION:             ______  (why would audiences want to go there?)
3. EXISTING IP OR ANCHOR:   ______  (book / song catalog / public-domain classic / trope)
4. CASTING:                 Lead A ______ (bankable)   Lead B ______ (rising)
5. SUPPORTING CAST (3+):    ______ / ______ / ______  (at least one parent or family figure)
6. MUSIC MOMENT:            ______  (the scene people will share on social media)
7. REASON FOR MEN TO COME:  ______
8. ROLES FOR VIEWERS 40+:   ______
9. MIDPOINT MOMENT:         ______
10. GRAND GESTURE:          ______

BUDGET TARGET:   $____M   (sweet spot $25–60M)
RELEASE WINDOW:  ______   (mid-Dec to Valentine's / early summer counter-programming)
COMPARABLE FILMS: ______ meets ______
SOCIAL MEDIA PLAN: ______  (which clip or trend launches the word of mouth?)

SCORECARD: ___ / 10   (greenlight at 8 or higher)
```

---

**Biggest takeaway:** the biggest hits weren't the funniest rom-coms. They delivered a vacation, a group outing and a soundtrack. Comedy and chemistry are necessary, but the films that broke out also sold a place and a shared experience.
